Growing Stronger

Building a strong first march, for Needles below Power Plant 30.

—

Intro

Welcome to the "Growing Stronger" section!

This section gives tips on how to grow stronger. It is for players with a power plant below level 30. At level 30, your strategy changes and you will need more than one strong march. We might follow up on that in a future section :-)

This tip follows one rule: make your first march as strong as possible. Give your other marches what remains. This series does not cover total battle power. That topic has its own series.

Note that these tips are not fixed rules. They come from our own experience playing the game, discussions with other players, and what we have been able to read in the game and some other guides we have found online. There may be better approaches, but we are certain that many of you can greatly increase your strength by following these tips. Particularly if you're a bit frustrated with your strength, or feel like you have no idea what to do.

The Bard understands. He has been there. Still is. If you disagree, or have other suggestions, let's discuss.

01

Heroes Explained 1: Tiers, Class & Faction

A hero has many characteristics. Today we explain the basics.

Tier. A hero has one of 3 tiers: R, SR or SSR.

R is the basic tier. Example heroes: Rusty, Ghost. R heroes have a blue portrait background. R heroes have weak skills, stats and weapons. R heroes cannot evolve. Use R heroes only if you have no other heroes. Replace R heroes as soon as possible.

SR is the middle tier. SR heroes are stronger than R heroes. SR heroes have better skills and stats. SR heroes cannot equip weapons. Some SR heroes can evolve to SSR. Example heroes: Chef, Sarge. SR heroes should form the backbone of your march early on. Replace SR heroes with SSR heroes over time.

SSR is the elite tier. SSR heroes have the best stats and skills. SSR heroes can equip special weapons. Replace your R and SR heroes with SSR heroes as you grow stronger.

Class. A hero has one of 3 classes: Guard (Shield), Gunner (Pistol) or Marksman (Rifle). The class decides which troops the hero can lead. The class also decides the bonus from reforged armour.

Faction. A hero has one of 4 factions: Stalwart (green shield), Aeronaut (light cloud), Rover (yellow) or Mariner (blue wave). The faction gives bonuses against specific enemies. The mix of factions in a march gives extra bonuses, explained in a later tip. Mariner heroes can act as any of the other 3 factions.

Aim for 5 SSR heroes in your first march. Use only one faction. Mix all 3 classes. If your first march is mostly R and SR heroes, upgrade this first.

02

Heroes Explained 2: Stars, Skills & Experience

Heroes grow through 3 resources: stars, skills and level. These resources are limited. Use them with care on the heroes in your first march. Stars and skills are locked to one hero once used. Experience can move freely between heroes.

Stars. A hero has 0 to 10 stars. Each star has 6 levels. Each full star increases stats like Attack, Defence and Health. Each full star also raises the maximum skill level. At specific star levels, like 8 and 10, all heroes in the march get a bonus.

Stars are raised using shards. Shards are specific to one hero, or generic to one tier (R, SR, SSR). Generic shards can convert to specific shards.

Skills. Skills are unique to each hero. A skill can boost the hero itself, for example a higher attack stat. A skill can boost all heroes in the march, for example restoring health. A skill can also be global, for example lowering the energy cost of rallies and attacks.

Higher tier heroes have more and better skills. SSR heroes have one special skill. This skill boosts all stats of the hero. It unlocks once all other skills reach level 30.

Level. Level defines how many escorts and troops a hero brings. Level increases through experience. The maximum level is capped by your power plant level.

How to prioritize. Focus stars, skills and experience on your first march. Do not spread them across many heroes.

Most players start with a few 10-star R heroes. As you get SSR heroes, replace an R hero with an SSR hero of the same faction and class. Example: replace Rusty with Nikola. Then collect shards specific to Nikola. Convert generic yellow shards to Nikola shards to raise his stars.

Aim for 10 stars on your first march heroes over time. Aim for 8 stars first, since this usually gives a march-wide bonus.

Focus skill growth on your first march heroes. Exception: global skills. Example: Lucky's skill "Wild Hunt" reduces stamina cost for all marches. Upgrade global skills even on heroes outside your first march.

Pick the heroes for your first march. Prioritize them. Focus stars, skills and experience on these heroes to raise the total power of your first march.

03

Heroes Explained 3: Armour, Upgrading, Enhancing & Reforging

You want the best possible armour for your first march. This means 5 full sets of upgraded, enhanced and reforged gold armour.

Grade. Armour comes in 5 grades: gold, purple, blue, green and white. Early on, use whatever grade you find. As you grow, focus on gold first, then purple.

Upgrade. Use gear scraps to upgrade your 5 gold sets as high as possible. Use lower grade armour to upgrade your purple armour. Keep your full 5 gold sets in your first march. Upgrade them evenly. In our experience, jackets first, then pants, then helmets works well. For example, raise all 5 jackets to level 20, then all 5 pants, then all 5 helmets, then repeat at level 30, and so on.

Enhance. Once you have 5 full gold sets, use extra gold armour to enhance the armour on your top 5 heroes. This sacrifices the extra armour, but raises the stars of the kept armour. Start with one gold jacket. Enhance it to 2 stars first. Then move to the next jacket, and repeat. At 4 and 6 stars, more bonuses unlock. We cover advanced enhancing and reforge anchors in a future tip.

Reforge. At 2 stars, you can reforge the armour using hammers. Reforging gives a random bonus. You can reforge as many times as you want, until you get a strong bonus. Look for a bonus that matches the hero's class. For example, a jacket with a troop defence bonus for Guard heroes should go to a Guard hero.

Priority order: upgrade your 5 gold sets, enhance them using extra gold armour, then reforge for strong, class-matched bonuses.

04

Heroes Explained 4: Weapons, Obtaining & Upgrading

Weapons are built using weapon fragments. Each fragment is specific to one hero. You need 10 fragments to build a hero's first weapon. Higher levels need more fragments.

How to get weapon fragments. Outside of paying real money, we know 3 sources.

  • Fight, and preferably win, in Reservoir Raid. This earns Reservoir Coins. Spend Reservoir Coins in the Reservoir store to buy Signature Gear Tokens.
  • In Season III, claim the final chest every day in Turbo Turtle. This gives 1 Signature Gear Token Fragment.
  • Reach rank 100 or higher in Turbo Turtle. This gives 1 or more Signature Gear Tokens.

Spend your Signature Gear Tokens and Token Fragments in the Hero Gear store. This buys weapon fragments for a specific hero.

How to prioritize. In our experience, the same rule as hero armour applies. Build the weapon for every SSR hero in your first march first. Then upgrade them one by one.

Some other guides argue the opposite: fully upgrade one hero's weapon before building the next. Their reasoning is that one strong weapon beats several half-built ones. Both approaches are valid. Pick the one that fits your resources and patience.

05

March Explained: Strength & Composition

A march has 5 heroes. Each hero has a faction and a class. The mix of both affects your march strength.

Faction and Final Health Bonus. Heroes sharing a faction give a Final Health Bonus. More heroes from the same faction gives a bigger bonus. For example, 5 heroes from the same faction gives a much higher bonus than 5 heroes split across different factions.

Mariner heroes are a wildcard. A Mariner counts as any of the other 3 factions. This makes them flexible, and they may also add other bonuses. In our experience, one faction across your full march gives the strongest health bonus.

Class. A march also needs a mix of classes: Guard, Gunner and Marksman. In our experience, 3 Guards, 1 Marksman and 1 Gunner works well.

Positioning. A march has 2 front row positions and 3 back row positions. The front row meets the enemy first. During battle, one hero from the back row also moves forward. Place your Guards in front, they protect your Gunner and Marksman from early damage. Place your Gunner and Marksman in the back, so they can deal damage safely.

Priority. Building the strongest possible march takes more than composition. Put your strongest heroes, all in gold armour, in your first march. Use remaining gold armour to enhance the armour of your first march. Keep your first march as your top priority, marches 2, 3 and 4 are useful, but not your main focus.

A word of warning If you have not done this before, your first march may get weaker for a while. Some strong heroes may need to be swapped out for the right faction or class fit. This is temporary. It pays off over time.

These faction and class recommendations come from our own experience, not confirmed testing. Other players hold different views. If you have tested this yourself, we want to hear about it.

06

Troops Explained: Why Troops Matter

A hero does not fight alone. Each hero leads troops in the march. Troops add to the total strength of the march.

Troop level matters. Higher level troops are stronger per unit than lower level troops. Troop count also matters. More troops in a march add more strength.

In our experience, many players focus only on heroes and forget their troops. This is a mistake. A march with strong heroes but weak or few troops is not as strong as it could be.

Train your troops at the highest level your buildings allow. Keep training queues full. Troop level is capped by your power plant level and other buildings, so this connects back to your base progression.

We do not have exact numbers on how much troops add to march strength compared to heroes. This is based on our own experience, not measured data. If you have tested this and found numbers, share them with us.

07

Chief Collection Explained

The Chief is your own avatar, not a hero. The Chief has its own equipment, called the Chief collection.

Chief collection equipment gives a permanent bonus to your whole account. Unlike hero armour, it does not belong to one hero or one march. It strengthens every march you have, including your first march and all others.

How it works. Chief collection items move through grades, shown by colour: white, green, blue, purple and gold. Within each grade, an item also has numbered levels. Chief collection items are upgraded using 2 resources: general parts and precision blueprints.

Level 1 of each item uses only general parts. You will collect plenty of these through normal play.

Higher levels need precision blueprints. Each level within green needs 10 blueprints. Promoting an item from green to blue needs 100 blueprints. Each new level within blue needs 30 blueprints. We cover how to obtain precision blueprints in a separate tip.

Priority. Since Chief collection is account-wide, in our experience it pays to raise all pieces evenly, rather than rushing one piece to a high level while the others stay low. A balanced set gives more total strength than one strong piece next to four weak ones.

We have not calculated this, so treat it as our own experience, not a confirmed rule.
